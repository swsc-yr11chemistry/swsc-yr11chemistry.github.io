Counting by weighing

Banks do not count every coin by hand. They weigh the bag, because every coin of the same type has the same mass. Chemists do the same thing with atoms, which are far too small and too many to count.

Your taskUse the mass of one item to count a large number of them.

Coins. One 20c coin has a mass of 11.3 g. A bag of 20c coins has a mass of 565 g. How many coins are in the bag?

Worked answer
number of coins = 565 g11.3 g = 50

Rice. One grain of rice has a mass of 0.025 g. How many grains are in a 100 g bag?

Worked answer
number of grains = 100 g0.025 g = 4000

Why do chemists count particles by weighing?

Worked answer
A drop of water has about 1.7 × 1021 molecules. Nobody could count them, but we can weigh them.

Year 10 recall. Find the molar mass of water, H2O. H = 1.0 and O = 16.0.

Worked answer
M(H2O) = 2 × 1.0 + 16.0 = 18.0 g/mol

Mass, moles and particles

The mole

In the warm up, you counted coins by weighing them. A mole is the chemist's counting unit, like a dozen but far bigger. One mole is 6.02 × 1023 particles. This number is called Avogadro's constant.

The number was chosen so that one mole of an element has a mass in grams equal to its relative atomic mass. One mole of carbon atoms has a mass of 12.0 g. This is the link that lets us count atoms by weighing them.

Show the answer

Molar mass of a compound

The molar mass (M) of a compound is the mass of one mole of it, in g/mol. Add up the relative atomic masses of every atom in the formula. A subscript tells you how many of that atom there are, and a number after a bracket multiplies everything inside.

Find M(CO2).M = 12.0 + 2 × 16.0 = 44.0 g/mol
Find M(Ca(OH)2).M = 40.1 + 2 × (16.0 + 1.0) = 74.1 g/molThe 2 after the bracket doubles both the O and the H.

Your turn. Find the molar mass of sulfuric acid, H2SO4.

Worked answer
M = 2 × 1.0 + 32.1 + 4 × 16.0 = 98.1 g/mol

From mass to moles

Now we can count by weighing. Divide the mass of a sample by the mass of one mole. This is the equation you rearranged in lesson 3.

n = mM, where n is the amount in mol, m is the mass in g, and M is the molar mass in g/mol.

How many moles are in 11.0 g of carbon dioxide?M(CO2) = 44.0 g/moln = mM = 11.0 g44.0 g/mol = 0.250 molg on the top and g in g/mol cancel, so the answer is in mol.

Your turn. How many moles are in 9.00 g of water? M(H2O) = 18.0 g/mol.

Worked answer
n = 9.00 g18.0 g/mol = 0.500 mol

From moles to mass

To weigh out a set number of moles, rearrange the equation. A chemist who needs 0.200 mol of salt for a reaction cannot count particles, but they can use a balance.

m = n × M

Find the mass of 0.200 mol of sodium chloride.M(NaCl) = 23.0 + 35.5 = 58.5 g/molm = n × M = 0.200 mol × 58.5 g/mol = 11.7 g

Your turn. Find the mass of 2.50 mol of magnesium.

Worked answer
m = n × M = 2.50 mol × 24.3 g/mol = 60.8 g

From moles to particles

Sometimes we need the actual number of particles. Multiply the moles by Avogadro's constant. We can also count the atoms inside each molecule, because the formula tells us how many of each atom one molecule has.

N = n × NA, where N is the number of particles and NA = 6.02 × 1023 mol−1.

How many molecules are in 0.500 mol of water? How many moles of H atoms?N = 0.500 mol × 6.02 × 1023 mol−1 = 3.01 × 1023 moleculesEach H2O has 2 H atoms, so n(H) = 2 × 0.500 = 1.00 mol of H atoms

Your turn. How many molecules are in 2.00 mol of carbon dioxide? Type it like 1.20e24.

Worked answer
N = 2.00 mol × 6.02 × 1023 mol−1 = 1.20 × 1024 molecules
