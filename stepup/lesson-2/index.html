Can you trust every digit?

A student finds the density of a liquid. They divide 2.0 g by 3.0 mL, and the calculator shows 0.666666667 g/mL. The calculator gives nine digits, but the balance and the measuring cylinder were nowhere near that precise. Some written results tell the truth about the equipment, and some do not.

Your taskFor each result, decide whether it is fair to write it like that, or whether it has too many digits for the equipment used. Then press Check my answers.

A lab balance shows 12.37 g. The student writes 12.37 g.

Fair. The balance shows two decimal places, so all four digits come from the measurement.

A ruler is marked in mm. The student writes 14.2386 cm.

Too many. A mm ruler can be read to about 0.01 cm at best, so 14.24 cm is the most you can claim.

The calculator gives 0.666666667 g/mL from 2.0 g and 3.0 mL.

Too many. The data had 2 significant figures, so the answer should be 0.67 g/mL.

A burette is read as 23.45 mL.

Fair. Burettes are read to the nearest 0.05 mL, so two decimal places is right.

A kitchen scale shows 250 g. The student writes 250.000 g.

Too many. The extra zeros claim a precision the kitchen scale does not have.

How many digits?

How many digits are in the reading 12.37 g?

Worked answer
There are 4 digits: 1, 2, 3 and 7. All of them came from the balance.

Which reading tells you more about the mass?

Worked answer

Counting and rounding significant figures

What significant figures tell us

In the warm up, some results had more digits than the equipment could measure. Every measurement has some digits we are sure of and one last digit that is estimated. Together these are the significant figures. A reading of 12.37 g has four significant figures, so it is more precise than a reading of 12 g, which has two.

Writing extra digits does not make a result better. It makes a false claim about the equipment. That is why chemists match the number of figures to the measurement.

Quick check. A thermometer reads 21.5 °C. How many significant figures is that?

Show the answer
3 significant figures: 2, 1 and 5.

Rules for counting

Most digits are easy to count. Zeros are the tricky part, because sometimes a zero is a measured digit and sometimes it only holds the place of the decimal point. Use these rules in order.

RuleExampleSignificant figures
All non-zero digits count.4.56 g3
Zeros between other digits count.4.05 g3
Leading zeros never count. They only show where the decimal point is.0.0045 g2
Trailing zeros after a decimal point count, because someone measured them.25.0 mL3
Trailing zeros in a whole number with no decimal point are unclear.1200 m2, 3 or 4

To make a whole number clear, write it in scientific notation. 1.20 × 10³ m has 3 significant figures, because every digit in the first number counts.

Quick check. How many significant figures are in 0.03040 g?

Worked answer
Leading zeros (0.0) do not count. The 3, the middle 0, the 4 and the final 0 all count, so there are 4 significant figures.

Rounding to significant figures

To round, count from the first significant figure to the last one you want to keep. Then look at the next digit. If it is 5 or more, round the last kept digit up. If it is less than 5, leave it.

Round 0.004567 to 2 significant figures.The first two significant figures are 4 and 5. The next digit is 6, so the 5 rounds up.0.004567 → 0.0046
Round 34 682 to 3 significant figures.Keep 3, 4 and 6. The next digit is 8, so the 6 rounds up to 7.34 682 → 34 700Fill the empty places with zeros so the number stays the same size. 347 would be far too small.
Round 2.996 to 3 significant figures.Keep 2, 9 and 9. The next digit is 6, so 2.99 rounds up to 3.00.2.996 → 3.00Write 3.00, not 3. The two zeros show that the value is known to 3 significant figures.

Significant figures in calculations

A calculated answer can only be as precise as the least precise measurement that went into it. When you multiply or divide, give the answer to the same number of significant figures as the data value with the fewest. Keep every digit in your calculator until the end, then round once.

A liquid has a mass of 12.37 g and a volume of 4.2 mL. Find its density.density = massvolume = 12.37 g4.2 mL = 2.945… g/mL12.37 has 4 significant figures and 4.2 has 2, so the answer gets 2.density = 2.9 g/mL

Both values have 3 significant figures, so the answer is 1.24 g/mL.

Scientific notation and significant figures

Very large and very small numbers are clearer in scientific notation. Every digit in the first number is significant, and the power of ten only sets the size. This removes any doubt about zeros.

NumberScientific notationSignificant figures
0.000 5205.20 × 10⁻⁴3
45 600 (to 3 figures)4.56 × 10⁴3
1500 (to 2 figures)1.5 × 10³2

Quick check. Write 72 300 to 2 significant figures. You can type it as a normal number or as 7.2e4.

Worked answer
Keep 7 and 2. The next digit is 3, so leave the 2.
72 300 → 72 000, which is 7.2 × 10⁴.
