Many everyday formulas link three quantities. If you know two of them, you can find the third. You have probably done this in your head without thinking about the equation.

Rearranging equations

An equation is a balance

In the warm up, you found litres by dividing the cost by the price. That works because an equation is like a balance. Both sides are equal, so if you do the same thing to both sides, they stay equal. This is the only rule you need to rearrange any equation.

A simple example12 = 3 × 4Divide both sides by 3: 123 = 3 × 43123 = 4Dividing by 3 undoes multiplying by 3. The 3 on the right cancels, so 4 is on its own.

You can always test a rearrangement with easy numbers like these. If the rearranged equation still gives a true statement, it is correct.

Quick check. 20 = 5 × 4. Rearrange it to get 5 on its own.

Show the answer
Divide both sides by 4: 5 = 204

Rearranging n = m over M

You used this equation in Year 10. It links the amount of a substance in mol (n), its mass in g (m) and its molar mass in g/mol (M). It answers three different questions, depending on which quantity you need.

n = mM

Make m the subject.Multiply both sides by M: n × M = mM × MThe M on the right cancels: m = n × M
Make M the subject.Start from m = n × M.Divide both sides by n: mn = MSo M = mnTest it: if m = 10 and n = 2, then M = 5. Check with the original: n = 105 = 2. True, so the rearrangement is right.

Quick check. Which equation gives M?

Worked answer
Starting from m = n × M, divide both sides by n. M = mn

Substituting with units

Once the equation is rearranged, put in the values with their units. Units cancel just like numbers, so they show whether your setup makes sense. If the units come out wrong, the rearrangement is probably wrong too.

Find the mass of 0.25 mol of sodium chloride. M = 58.5 g/mol.m = n × Mm = 0.25 mol × 58.5 g/mol = 14.6 gThe data value 0.25 has 2 significant figures, so m = 15 g.mol on top and mol on the bottom of g/mol cancel, leaving g. A mass in g is what we wanted.

Your turn. 9.8 g of a substance is 0.10 mol. Find its molar mass.

Worked answer
M = mn = 9.8 g0.10 mol = 98 g/mol

Density

Density tells us how much mass is packed into each mL or cm³ of a substance. Gold feels heavy for its size because it has a high density. The equation has the same shape as n = m over M, so it rearranges the same way.

d = mV, so m = d × V and V = md

A block of aluminium has a mass of 27.0 g. Its density is 2.70 g/cm³. Find its volume.V = md = 27.0 g2.70 g/cm³ = 10.0 cm³

Your turn. Ethanol has a density of 0.789 g/mL. Find the mass of 50.0 mL of ethanol.

Worked answer
m = d × V = 0.789 g/mL × 50.0 mL = 39.5 g

Percentages

Food labels, fertilisers and medicines often give a percentage. A percentage compares a part with the whole, then multiplies by 100. You can rearrange it to find the part or the whole.

percentage = partwhole × 100

A 250 g packet of cereal is 12% sugar. Find the mass of sugar.part = percentage100 × wholepart = 12100 × 250 g = 30 g
3.6 g of salt is 18% of a sample. Find the mass of the whole sample.whole = part × 100percentagewhole = 3.6 g × 10018 = 20 g

Your turn. A 40.0 g sample contains 6.0 g of salt. What percentage of the sample is salt?

Worked answer
percentage = 6.0 g40.0 g × 100 = 15%
