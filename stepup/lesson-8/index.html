Reading continuous data

The parts of a graph

In the warm up, time was along the bottom and temperature went up the side. That layout is standard. The independent variable is the one the scientist chooses or changes, and it goes on the horizontal (x) axis. The dependent variable is the one that is measured, and it goes on the vertical (y) axis. Each axis label gives the quantity and its unit.

In Year 11 and 12, most graphs show continuous data. Both variables can take any value, so the points are joined by a line or a smooth curve.

Quick check. In this graph, what is the independent variable?

Worked answer
Time is chosen by the scientist and is on the x axis, so it is the independent variable.

Reading values

To read a value, start on the axis you know. Go straight to the line, then straight across or down to the other axis. Before you start, work out what each grid square is worth. Always write the answer with the unit from the axis label.

Describing a trend

A trend is the overall pattern. A good description says what happens to y as x increases, and how it happens. Is the change steady, speeding up or slowing down? Does it level off? Then link the shape to the chemistry if you can.

Describe the trend in the CO2 graph.As time increases, the volume of CO2 increases at a steady rate until 40 s.After 40 s, the volume stays at 60 mL.The line is flat after 40 s because one of the reactants has been used up, so no more gas forms.

Gradient

The gradient tells us how steep a line is. On a graph of amount against time, the gradient is the rate, which tells us how fast something happens. To find the gradient of a straight line, pick two points on it that are far apart. Divide the change in y by the change in x, and include the units.

gradient = change in ychange in x

Find the gradient of the CO2 line from 0 s to 40 s.gradient = 60 mL − 0 mL40 s − 0 s = 1.5 mL/sThe gas is made at 1.5 mL every second.

Quick check. What is the gradient of the CO2 line from 40 s to 80 s?

Worked answer
The volume does not change, so gradient = 60 mL − 60 mL80 s − 40 s = 0 mL/s. No more gas is being made.

Calibration curves

Chemists often find an unknown concentration with a calibration curve. First they measure a set of standard solutions whose concentrations are known. For a coloured solution, they measure its absorbance, which is how much light it absorbs. They plot the standards, then measure the unknown sample and read its concentration off the graph.

A water sample has an absorbance of 0.50. What is its concentration?Go across from 0.50 to the line, then down.Concentration = 125 mg/L

Reading between the standards, as here, is called interpolation, and it is reliable. Reading beyond the last standard is called extrapolation. It is less reliable, because we have no data to show the line stays straight out there.

Your turn. A sample has an absorbance of 0.30. What is its concentration?

Worked answer
Go across from 0.30 to the line, then down. Concentration = 75 mg/L.
