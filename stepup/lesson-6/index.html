Ratios you already use

A recipe tells you how much of each ingredient goes together. If you double the recipe, every ingredient doubles. A balanced equation is a recipe for a reaction, and its numbers work the same way.

Your taskUse the ratio to scale each recipe up.

Pancakes. A recipe uses 2 cups of flour for every 3 eggs. How many eggs do you need for 8 cups of flour?

Worked answer
eggs = 8 cups × 3 eggs2 cups = 12 eggs

Car factory. Each car needs 4 wheels. How many cars can the factory build from 100 wheels?

Worked answer
cars = 100 wheels × 1 car4 wheels = 25 cars

In 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O, how many molecules of water form from 10 molecules of hydrogen?

Worked answer
The ratio of H2 to H2O is 2 : 2, which is 1 : 1. So 10 H2 make 10 H2O.

Does 2 g of hydrogen react with 1 g of oxygen in the same reaction?

Worked answer
The numbers in front count particles or moles. To work with grams, we must convert to moles first. That is today's method.

The mass-to-mass method

Numbers in an equation are mole ratios

In the warm up, the recipe ratio told you how many eggs to use. In a balanced equation, the numbers in front tell you how many particles react. Because a mole is a fixed number of particles, they also tell you how many moles react. This is the mole ratio.

The Haber process makes ammonia.N2 + 3H2 → 2NH31 mol of N2 reacts with 3 mol of H2 to make 2 mol of NH3.

The ratio holds for any amount. 10 mol of N2 would react with 30 mol of H2 to make 20 mol of NH3.

Quick check. How many moles of NH3 form from 6 mol of H2?

Worked answer
H2 : NH3 is 3 : 2. n(NH3) = 6 mol × 23 = 4 mol

Using the mole ratio

To change moles of one substance into moles of another, multiply by a fraction made from the equation. Put the number in front of the substance you want on top, and the number in front of the substance you know on the bottom. This is the same idea as the conversion factors in lesson 1.

n(wanted) = n(known) × number in front of wantednumber in front of known

How many moles of NH3 form from 0.30 mol of N2?n(NH3) = 0.30 mol × 21 = 0.60 mol

Your turn. How many moles of NH3 form from 1.5 mol of H2?

Worked answer
n(NH3) = 1.5 mol × 23 = 1.0 mol

The four-step method

We cannot go straight from grams of one substance to grams of another, because different particles have different masses. Instead, we go through moles. The same four steps work for every mass-to-mass question.

What mass of magnesium oxide forms when 4.86 g of magnesium burns?Step 1. 2Mg + O2 → 2MgOStep 2. n(Mg) = 4.86 g24.3 g/mol = 0.200 molStep 3. n(MgO) = 0.200 mol × 22 = 0.200 molStep 4. m(MgO) = 0.200 mol × 40.3 g/mol = 8.06 gStep 2 uses the molar mass of Mg. Step 4 uses the molar mass of MgO.

A ratio that is not 1 : 1

In the magnesium example, the ratio was 1 : 1, so the moles stayed the same. Often the ratio is different, and step 3 changes the number of moles. This is where most mistakes happen, so write the fraction out every time.

Methane burns in a gas stove. What mass of water forms from 8.0 g of methane?Step 1. CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2OStep 2. n(CH4) = 8.0 g16.0 g/mol = 0.50 molStep 3. n(H2O) = 0.50 mol × 21 = 1.0 molStep 4. m(H2O) = 1.0 mol × 18.0 g/mol = 18 g

Your turn. What mass of carbon dioxide forms from the same 8.0 g of methane? M(CO2) = 44.0 g/mol.

Worked answer
n(CH4) = 0.50 mol
n(CO2) = 0.50 mol × 11 = 0.50 mol
m(CO2) = 0.50 mol × 44.0 g/mol = 22 g

Working out how much reactant you need

Industry often works backwards. A factory knows how much product it wants, and needs to know how much reactant to buy. The four steps are the same, but you start from the product.

How much hydrogen is needed to make 34.0 g of ammonia? N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3Step 2. n(NH3) = 34.0 g17.0 g/mol = 2.00 molStep 3. n(H2) = 2.00 mol × 32 = 3.00 molStep 4. m(H2) = 3.00 mol × 2.0 g/mol = 6.0 g

Your turn. What mass of nitrogen is needed to make the same 34.0 g of ammonia? M(N2) = 28.0 g/mol.

Worked answer
n(NH3) = 2.00 mol
n(N2) = 2.00 mol × 12 = 1.00 mol
m(N2) = 1.00 mol × 28.0 g/mol = 28.0 g

Stoichiometry in industry and the lab

Use the four steps for every question. Molar masses are given. Give answers to 3 significant figures.

Core questions

Making iron. A blast furnace runs Fe2O3 + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO2. What mass of iron forms from 320 g of Fe2O3? M(Fe2O3) = 159.6 g/mol, M(Fe) = 55.8 g/mol.

Worked answer
n(Fe2O3) = 320 g159.6 g/mol = 2.005 mol
n(Fe) = 2.005 mol × 21 = 4.010 mol
m(Fe) = 4.010 mol × 55.8 g/mol = 224 g

Baking. Baking soda breaks down in the oven: 2NaHCO3 → Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2. What mass of CO2 forms from 8.40 g of NaHCO3? M(NaHCO3) = 84.0 g/mol.

Worked answer
n(NaHCO3) = 8.40 g84.0 g/mol = 0.100 mol
n(CO2) = 0.100 mol × 12 = 0.0500 mol
m(CO2) = 0.0500 mol × 44.0 g/mol = 2.20 g

Antacid. Mg(OH)2 + 2HCl → MgCl2 + 2H2O. What mass of MgCl2 forms from 5.83 g of Mg(OH)2? M(Mg(OH)2) = 58.3 g/mol, M(MgCl2) = 95.3 g/mol.

Worked answer
n(Mg(OH)2) = 5.83 g58.3 g/mol = 0.100 mol
n(MgCl2) = 0.100 mol × 11 = 0.100 mol
m(MgCl2) = 0.100 mol × 95.3 g/mol = 9.53 g

Fertiliser. N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3. What mass of ammonia forms from 14.0 g of nitrogen?

Worked answer
n(N2) = 14.0 g28.0 g/mol = 0.500 mol
n(NH3) = 0.500 mol × 21 = 1.00 mol
m(NH3) = 1.00 mol × 17.0 g/mol = 17.0 g

Barbecue. C3H8 + 5O2 → 3CO2 + 4H2O. What mass of CO2 forms from 4.40 g of propane? M(C3H8) = 44.0 g/mol.

Worked answer
n(C3H8) = 4.40 g44.0 g/mol = 0.100 mol
n(CO2) = 0.100 mol × 31 = 0.300 mol
m(CO2) = 0.300 mol × 44.0 g/mol = 13.2 g

Barbecue. What mass of oxygen is needed to burn that 4.40 g of propane? M(O2) = 32.0 g/mol.

Worked answer
n(C3H8) = 0.100 mol
n(O2) = 0.100 mol × 51 = 0.500 mol
m(O2) = 0.500 mol × 32.0 g/mol = 16.0 g

Show what you know

Choose one answer for each question. You will see the worked answer straight away.

1. In 2C2H6 + 7O2 → 4CO2 + 6H2O, what is the mole ratio of O2 to CO2?

Worked answer
Read the numbers in front: 7 O2 and 4 CO2, so the ratio is 7 : 4.

2. In 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O, 0.60 mol of O2 reacts. How many moles of water form?

Worked answer
n(H2O) = 0.60 mol × 21 = 1.2 mol

3. What is the right order for a mass-to-mass calculation?

Worked answer
Balance, then mass to moles, moles to moles, moles to mass.

4. In 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O, what mass of water forms from 4.0 g of hydrogen?

Worked answer
n(H2) = 4.0 g2.0 g/mol = 2.0 mol. n(H2O) = 2.0 mol. m = 2.0 × 18.0 = 36 g.

5. A student says 8 g of CH4 makes 8 g of CO2, because the ratio in CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O is 1 : 1. What is wrong?

Worked answer
0.50 mol of CH4 makes 0.50 mol of CO2, which is 22 g, not 8 g. The ratio is in moles.

6. Limestone breaks down when heated: CaCO3 → CaO + CO2. What mass of CaO forms from 10.0 g of CaCO3? M(CaCO3) = 100.1 g/mol, M(CaO) = 56.1 g/mol.

Worked answer
n(CaCO3) = 10.0 g100.1 g/mol = 0.0999 mol. n(CaO) = 0.0999 mol. m = 0.0999 × 56.1 = 5.60 g.
