Atoms and ions

An aluminium ion is written as ²⁷Al³⁺. The atomic number of aluminium is 13.

1. How many protons does this ion have?

Worked answer
The atomic number tells us the number of protons. Aluminium is element 13, so it has 13 protons.

2. How many neutrons does this ion have?

Worked answer
Neutrons = mass number − atomic number = 27 − 13 = 14 neutrons.

3. How many electrons does this ion have?

Worked answer
A neutral aluminium atom has 13 electrons. The 3+ charge means it has lost 3 electrons, so it has 13 − 3 = 10 electrons.

Ionic compounds and equations

4. What is the formula of the ionic compound formed by Ca²⁺ ions and NO₃⁻ ions?

Worked answer
The charges must add up to zero. Ca²⁺ has a charge of 2+, and each NO₃⁻ has a charge of 1−, so we need two nitrate ions. Brackets go around the polyatomic ion: Ca(NO₃)₂.

5. Silver nitrate solution is mixed with sodium chloride solution, and a white precipitate of silver chloride forms. Which is the ionic equation for this reaction?

Worked answer
Only the silver ions and the chloride ions react to form the solid precipitate. The sodium ions and nitrate ions stay dissolved, so they are spectator ions and are left out. The ionic equation is Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s).

Moles

6. Calculate the amount, in mol, of water in 9.0 g of water. The molar mass of water is 18.0 g mol⁻¹.

Worked answer
n = mM = 9.0 g18.0 g mol⁻¹ = 0.50 mol
Here n is the amount in mol, m is the mass in g and M is the molar mass in g mol⁻¹.

What Year 11 Chemistry covers

How the course is organised

Year 11 Chemistry is made up of Unit 1 and Unit 2 of VCE Chemistry. Unit 1 runs from the start of Term 1 until the middle of Term 2. Unit 2 runs from Term 2 until the end of the year.

Each unit is built around a big question and is split into three areas of study, often shortened to AOS. Each area of study has a list of key knowledge you need to learn, and each one ends with a SAC.

Your Unit 1 and 2 results are not part of your Year 12 study score. Everything you learn this year is assumed knowledge in Units 3 and 4, though, so the work you do now sets you up for Year 12.

Unit 1

The big question for Unit 1 is How can the diversity of materials be explained? Unit 1 looks at why different materials behave so differently. You will see how the bonding inside a substance explains its properties, then learn to count particles using the mole.

The three types of SAC

Written SAC

You do it in class under test conditions. It has multiple-choice and short-answer questions, including calculations. It can test any key knowledge from that area of study, so revise all of it, not only the parts you like.

Research investigation

You research a question about chemistry and sustainability, use reliable sources, and present what you find in the format your teacher sets.

Investigation

You adapt or design an experiment, collect your own data, and write a report that analyses and evaluates your results.

Answer the question that is asked

Each question starts with a command word that tells you what kind of answer to give. Getting this right is an easy way to earn marks.

Command wordWhat it asks you to do
State or identifyGive one fact or one point. No reason is needed.
DescribeSay what something is like or what happens.
ExplainSay why or how something happens, and link the cause to the chemistry.
CalculateShow each line of working, and give the answer with a unit and the right number of significant figures.
State one property of ionic compounds.They have high melting points.One point is enough for a "state" question.
Explain why molten sodium chloride conducts electricity.When sodium chloride melts, its ions are free to move. Moving ions can carry charge through the liquid, so it conducts electricity.An "explain" answer gives the reason and links it to the particles.

How to do well this year

  • Bring your textbook to every lesson.
  • Do the chapter questions every week, not the night before a SAC.
  • On lesson pages, try each question before you open the worked answer.
  • Ask for help as soon as something does not make sense. Topics build on each other, so a gap early on grows later.
  • Start revising at the beginning of each area of study, not the week of the SAC.
  • Use the course outline to see what is coming up each week and to find lesson materials.

Worked answer
An "explain" answer gives the reason and links it to the particles. The ions are free to move when molten, so they can carry charge.

Worked answer
n = cV links the amount of a chemical to the concentration and volume of a solution. You will learn it in Unit 2 AOS 2.
